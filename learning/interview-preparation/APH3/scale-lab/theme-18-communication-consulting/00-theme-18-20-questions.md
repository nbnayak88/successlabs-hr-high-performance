### HR-APH3-B18-Q01 — Explaining a Complex Design Simply
### Interview Question
How would you explain a complex Performance & Goals architecture to a non-technical HR leader?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** HR leaders understood the business problem but not the technical dependencies behind the proposed solution.

**Task:** I needed to enable an informed decision without overwhelming them with technical detail.

**Action:** I translated the architecture into employee, manager, HR and business journeys, explained the major dependencies in business language, and used a simple current-state versus target-state view.

**Result:** The stakeholders understood the trade-offs and made the required design decision confidently.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would explain how goals, feedback, performance forms, workflows, calibration, security, reporting and Employee Central dependencies work together rather than starting with configuration terminology.

**SME Probe:** How do you know whether your explanation was actually understood?

### HR-APH3-B18-Q02 — Consulting Through Discovery
### Interview Question
How would you conduct discovery when a client asks you to “implement Performance Management”?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client had already selected the product but had not clearly defined the business problem.

**Task:** I needed to avoid jumping directly into configuration.

**Action:** I explored current performance processes, pain points, employee and manager journeys, policy, data, integrations, governance and desired outcomes before defining the solution scope.

**Result:** The program moved from product implementation toward an outcome-based transformation.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would discover how the organization manages goals, feedback, reviews, calibration and performance evidence before designing the SuccessFactors solution.

**SME Probe:** What question would you ask before discussing any configuration?

### HR-APH3-B18-Q04 — Asking Better Questions
### Interview Question
What questions would you ask when a client says their Performance & Goals process is “not working”?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The client described the issue only as low satisfaction.

**Task:** I needed to convert a vague complaint into diagnosable problems.

**Action:** I asked about affected personas, process stages, frequency, business impact, adoption, data, workflow, system behavior and desired outcomes.

**Result:** The discussion moved from opinion to evidence and produced actionable problem statements.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would separate goal-setting, feedback, review, calibration, reporting and user-experience issues before proposing remediation.

**SME Probe:** Which question helps you distinguish a process problem from a technology problem?

### HR-APH3-B18-Q05 — Challenging the Client
### Interview Question
How would you challenge a client who insists on reproducing its legacy performance process?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The client wanted the new platform to reproduce every legacy step.

**Task:** I needed to challenge unnecessary complexity while respecting legitimate business requirements.

**Action:** I mapped each legacy step to its intended outcome, identified obsolete controls and duplicated activities, and demonstrated a simpler target process.

**Result:** The client agreed to modernize rather than simply digitize the legacy process.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would use standard Performance & Goals capabilities where they support the desired outcome and reserve customization for justified gaps.

**SME Probe:** When should a legacy requirement be retained?

### HR-APH3-B18-Q06 — Communicating Trade-Offs
### Interview Question
How would you communicate a trade-off between functionality and maintainability?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A stakeholder requested a highly customized performance workflow that would increase long-term maintenance.

**Task:** I needed to make the future impact visible.

**Action:** I compared short-term user value with configuration complexity, support effort, upgrade impact and future change cost.

**Result:** The stakeholder selected a simpler option with an acceptable business trade-off.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would compare standard workflow and configuration patterns against customization before recommending a design.

**SME Probe:** How would you quantify maintainability to a business stakeholder?

### HR-APH3-B18-Q07 — Handling Ambiguous Requirements
### Interview Question
How would you consult when the client requirement is ambiguous?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A requirement stated that managers should have “full visibility” into employee performance.

**Task:** I needed to clarify what visibility actually meant.

**Action:** I decomposed the requirement by data type, employee population, timing, business purpose and persona, then converted it into testable statements.

**Result:** The requirement became precise enough for architecture, configuration and security design.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would distinguish visibility of goals, ratings, feedback, comments and historical information instead of treating “full visibility” as a single permission.

**SME Probe:** What makes a requirement testable?

### HR-APH3-B18-Q08 — Advisory vs Configuration
### Interview Question
How would you prevent consulting conversations from becoming configuration discussions too early?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Workshops repeatedly moved into field-level configuration before the process was agreed.

**Task:** I needed to keep the conversation outcome-focused.

**Action:** I established layers: business outcome, process, experience, information, architecture and only then configuration. I parked detailed configuration questions until the appropriate design stage.

**Result:** Decisions became more coherent and rework decreased.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would agree the performance-management model before deciding individual form fields or configuration options.

**SME Probe:** Why can early configuration discussions create architectural debt?

**Task:** I needed evidence rather than subjective opinions.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would use review-cycle metrics, goal completion, feedback usage, support demand and calibration timing to inform recommendations.

**SME Probe:** What would you do if quantitative and qualitative evidence conflict?

### HR-APH3-B18-Q12 — Consulting Across Business and IT
### Interview Question
How would you bridge the language gap between HR and IT teams?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** HR discussed performance policy while IT discussed configuration and interfaces, with neither side fully understanding the other.

**Task:** I needed to create a shared model.

**Action:** I used process journeys, personas, data flows and decision points as common language, then connected those concepts to technical architecture.

**Result:** HR and IT could collaborate around the same problem definition.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would trace a business requirement from performance policy to process, SuccessFactors configuration, security, integration and reporting.

**SME Probe:** Why is a process model often more useful than a product feature list?

### HR-APH3-B18-Q13 — Saying No Professionally
### Interview Question
How would you say no to a client request that creates unacceptable architectural risk?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client requested a shortcut that would weaken security and create future maintenance risk.

**Task:** I needed to protect the solution without damaging the consulting relationship.

**Action:** I explained the business consequence, offered safer alternatives and clearly distinguished the recommendation from the final client decision.

**Result:** The client accepted an alternative that met the business objective without the same architectural risk.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would avoid designs that bypass RBP, expose sensitive performance information or create uncontrolled custom dependencies.

**SME Probe:** What makes a refusal credible rather than obstructive?
