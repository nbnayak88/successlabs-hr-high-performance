### HR-APH3-B01-Q01 — Performance Management as a Business Capability

**Interview Question:** How would you explain Performance & Goals as a business capability rather than an HR system?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** An organization viewed performance management primarily as an annual appraisal process.

**Task:** I needed to reposition it as a business capability that improves workforce alignment and outcomes.

**Action:** I connected strategy, role expectations, goals, continuous feedback, evidence, review, calibration, development and business outcomes.

**Result:** Leadership could see performance management as an ongoing mechanism for translating strategy into employee outcomes.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would position goals and performance forms as enabling capabilities within a broader performance operating model rather than treating the application as the process itself.

**SME Probe:** What makes performance management a strategic capability?

---

### HR-APH3-B01-Q02 — Goals and Strategy Alignment

**Interview Question:** How would you ensure employee goals align with organizational strategy?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Employees were setting technically valid goals that did not clearly contribute to strategic priorities.

**Task:** I needed to improve vertical and horizontal goal alignment.

**Action:** I established cascading or aligned goals, clear organizational priorities, role expectations and measurable outcomes, while allowing appropriate individual contribution.

**Result:** Employees could understand how their objectives contributed to broader business priorities.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** SuccessFactors Goal Management can support aligned objectives while Performance Management captures evidence and evaluation against those expectations.

**SME Probe:** What is the danger of mechanically cascading goals?

---

### HR-APH3-B01-Q03 — Annual Appraisal vs Continuous Performance

**Interview Question:** How would you transform an annual appraisal process into continuous performance management?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers and employees interacted mainly during the annual review cycle.

**Task:** I needed to create a more continuous performance dialogue.

**Action:** I introduced goal check-ins, ongoing feedback, evidence capture, manager conversations and periodic review points while retaining formal evaluation where required.

**Result:** Performance became a continuous management practice instead of a once-a-year event.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal Management, Performance Management and Continuous Performance capabilities can support an ongoing performance conversation.

**SME Probe:** How would you prevent continuous performance from becoming administrative overload?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q04 — Performance Evidence

**Interview Question:** What constitutes good performance evidence?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Performance ratings were sometimes based on manager memory or recent events.

**Task:** I needed to improve the quality and fairness of performance evidence.

**Action:** I encouraged measurable outcomes, behavioral evidence, documented feedback, goal progress and relevant business context throughout the performance cycle.

**Result:** Reviews became more evidence-based and less dependent on recency or subjective recollection.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance forms can consolidate goals, competencies, comments and feedback to support a structured review.

**SME Probe:** How would you reduce recency bias?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q05 — Performance and Development

**Interview Question:** How should performance management connect with employee development?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Performance reviews identified capability gaps but the organization rarely translated them into development actions.

**Task:** I needed to close the loop between evaluation and growth.

**Action:** I linked performance feedback, competency gaps, career aspirations and development actions, while keeping ownership clear across performance and development processes.

**Result:** Performance conversations became inputs to employee growth rather than simply rating events.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance outcomes can inform development discussions while AGL4 remains responsible for broader succession and development architecture.

### HR-APH3-B01-Q06 — Rating Philosophy

**Interview Question:** How would you establish a sound performance-rating philosophy?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers applied ratings inconsistently across teams.

**Task:** I needed to improve consistency without eliminating managerial judgment.

**Action:** I defined rating dimensions, evidence expectations, behavioral anchors, calibration practices and manager guidance.

**Result:** Ratings became more consistent and defensible.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Rating scales, competencies and performance-form design should reflect the organization's approved performance philosophy.

**SME Probe:** Why should calibration not be used simply to force a distribution?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q07 — Goals vs Competencies

**Interview Question:** What is the difference between evaluating goals and evaluating competencies?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers treated achievement of targets as the complete definition of performance.

**Task:** I needed to establish a balanced performance model.

**Action:** I distinguished **what** was achieved through goals from **how** work was performed through competencies and behaviors.

**Result:** Performance evaluation became more comprehensive and aligned with organizational values.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance forms can evaluate goals alongside competencies or other approved performance dimensions.

**SME Probe:** Can someone achieve all goals and still receive a poor performance outcome? Why?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q08 — Goal Quality

**Interview Question:** How would you improve poor-quality employee goals?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Many goals were vague, activity-based or impossible to measure.

**Task:** I needed to improve goal quality without making goal setting unnecessarily complex.

**Action:** I coached managers and employees on outcome orientation, measurable indicators, timeframes, ownership and strategic relevance.

**Result:** Goals became clearer and performance conversations became more meaningful.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal plans should encourage measurable outcomes and appropriate alignment rather than simply recording task lists.

**SME Probe:** What is the difference between an activity and an outcome?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q09 — Manager Accountability

**Interview Question:** How would you improve manager accountability for performance management?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers often delayed goal discussions and reviews until deadlines approached.

**Task:** I needed to make managers active owners of performance rather than form approvers.

**Action:** I clarified manager responsibilities, introduced checkpoints, monitored completion and quality indicators, and reinforced the expectation of meaningful performance conversations.

**Result:** Manager participation improved and performance became more integrated into normal leadership practice.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Workflow and reporting can support completion tracking, but accountability remains an operating-model responsibility.

**SME Probe:** Can technology create manager accountability by itself?

**Situation:** Employees perceived performance management as something done to them by managers.

**Task:** I needed to make employees active participants.

**Action:** I emphasized goal ownership, self-reflection, progress updates, feedback requests and development conversations.

**Result:** Employees became more engaged in managing their performance journey.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Employee self-assessments, goal updates and continuous feedback can support employee participation.

### HR-APH3-B01-Q11 — Calibration

**Interview Question:** Why is calibration important in performance management?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Similar performance produced materially different ratings across teams.

**Task:** I needed to improve consistency and fairness.

**Action:** I introduced structured calibration conversations using evidence, role context and agreed rating principles rather than arbitrary ranking.

**Result:** Leadership gained greater confidence in rating consistency.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Calibration capabilities can support structured review of ratings across teams while preserving appropriate managerial accountability.

**SME Probe:** What is the difference between calibration and forced ranking?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q12 — Bias in Performance

**Interview Question:** How would you address bias in performance evaluations?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Analysis showed potential differences in ratings across employee groups.

**Task:** I needed to understand and reduce systemic bias.

**Action:** I examined rating patterns, evidence quality, manager practices, competency definitions and calibration behavior, then introduced manager guidance and monitoring.

**Result:** The organization had a more evidence-based and transparent performance process.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance data can support monitoring, but bias mitigation requires process, governance and leadership intervention beyond configuration.

**SME Probe:** What kinds of bias are common in performance reviews?

### HR-APH3-B01-Q15 — Performance and Compensation Boundary

**Interview Question:** How would you explain the relationship between Performance & Goals and Compensation?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Stakeholders wanted to embed compensation decisions directly into performance processes.

**Task:** I needed to clarify the architectural relationship.

### HR-APH3-B01-Q16 — Performance and Succession Boundary

**Interview Question:** How can performance information support succession without making performance the same as potential?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Leaders assumed the highest performers should automatically become successors.

**Task:** I needed to clarify the relationship between performance and succession.

**Action:** I explained that performance evidence can inform talent decisions, but potential, readiness, aspirations, role requirements and development also matter.

**Result:** Succession decisions became more balanced and less dependent on historical performance alone.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** APH3 provides performance evidence; AGL4 owns succession and development decisions and broader talent architecture.

**SME Probe:** Why is high performance not equivalent to high potential?
