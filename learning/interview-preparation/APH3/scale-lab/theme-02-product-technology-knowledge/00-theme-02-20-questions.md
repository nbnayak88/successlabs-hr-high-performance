### HR-APH3-B02-Q01 — Performance & Goals Product Architecture

**Interview Question:** How would you explain the main SuccessFactors Performance & Goals capabilities to a client?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client knew the product name but not how its capabilities supported the performance operating model.

**Task:** I needed to explain the platform in business terms.

**Action:** I grouped capabilities around goal management, performance forms, competencies, continuous feedback, performance reviews, calibration, reporting and related employee experiences.

**Result:** The client understood the platform as a set of capabilities supporting an end-to-end performance journey rather than a collection of screens.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal Management supports objectives; Performance Management supports formal evaluation; Continuous Performance supports ongoing dialogue; Calibration supports consistency.

**SME Probe:** Which capabilities would you consider foundational before advanced performance analytics?

### HR-APH3-B02-Q02 — Goal Management

**Interview Question:** What role does Goal Management play in an enterprise performance architecture?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Employees were maintaining goals outside the HR platform and entering them manually during reviews.

**Task:** I needed to establish a governed goal-management capability.

**Action:** I defined goal structures, ownership, alignment, measurement, lifecycle and integration with performance reviews.

**Result:** Goals became reusable performance information rather than isolated planning documents.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal Management can provide structured goals that are referenced within Performance Management processes.

**SME Probe:** What makes a goal model scalable across an enterprise?

### HR-APH3-B02-Q03 — Performance Forms

**Interview Question:** How would you design a Performance Management form without turning it into a policy document?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client had a very long appraisal form containing every possible HR requirement.

**Task:** I needed to preserve governance while improving usability.

**Action:** I separated essential performance decisions from supporting information, simplified sections, clarified rating criteria and tested the journey with managers and employees.

**Result:** The form became easier to complete while retaining necessary evaluation controls.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance forms should focus on meaningful goals, competencies, evidence, ratings and comments rather than reproducing every policy statement.

**SME Probe:** What is your first principle when deciding whether a field belongs on a performance form?

### HR-APH3-B02-Q04 — Competency Architecture

**Interview Question:** How would you use competencies within SuccessFactors Performance & Goals?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization measured only target achievement and had limited visibility into behaviors and capabilities.

**Task:** I needed to introduce a balanced performance view.

**Action:** I defined competency categories, behavioral expectations, rating guidance and their relationship to the organization's performance philosophy.

**Result:** Performance evaluation captured both outcomes and behaviors.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Competencies can be incorporated into performance forms and evaluated alongside goals.

**SME Probe:** How would you avoid creating hundreds of competencies that managers cannot meaningfully use?

### HR-APH3-B02-Q05 — Continuous Performance

**Interview Question:** How would you position Continuous Performance capabilities in a traditional annual-review organization?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers had meaningful performance conversations only once a year.

**Task:** I needed to introduce continuous performance without disrupting formal governance.

**Action:** I used continuous feedback, check-ins and progress conversations as complementary mechanisms, while retaining formal review milestones.

**Result:** Employees received more timely feedback and managers had stronger evidence for formal reviews.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Continuous Performance capabilities can support ongoing feedback and conversations that complement formal Performance Management forms.

**SME Probe:** What should be the relationship between continuous feedback and the annual performance form?

### HR-APH3-B02-Q06 — Performance Rating Scales

**Interview Question:** How would you choose an appropriate rating scale?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization had inconsistent rating scales across business units.

**Task:** I needed to establish a coherent enterprise approach.

**Action:** I aligned the scale to the performance philosophy, defined behavioral or outcome anchors, tested interpretation with managers and assessed reporting implications.

**Result:** Ratings became more consistent and easier to interpret.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** SuccessFactors rating scales should reflect approved enterprise performance policy and be applied consistently through governed configuration.

**SME Probe:** Why is a five-point scale not automatically better than a three-point scale?

### HR-APH3-B02-Q07 — Goal Cascading and Alignment

**Interview Question:** How would you configure goal alignment without encouraging mechanical goal cascading?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Leaders wanted every employee goal to cascade directly from executive goals.

**Task:** I needed to preserve strategic alignment while allowing meaningful individual contribution.

**Action:** I distinguished enterprise priorities from role-specific outcomes and allowed alignment through contribution, shared goals and appropriate individual objectives.

**Result:** Employees could connect their work to strategy without copying executive goals.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal plans can support aligned objectives while retaining role-appropriate goal ownership.

**SME Probe:** What is the difference between goal alignment and goal duplication?

**Situation:** Leadership wanted greater consistency in performance ratings across managers.

**Task:** I needed to support evidence-based calibration.

**Action:** I established calibration participants, data visibility, rating criteria, discussion rules and governance before configuring the technology.

**Result:** Calibration became a structured decision process rather than a technology-driven ranking exercise.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Calibration can help leaders review performance ratings across populations and identify inconsistencies.

### HR-APH3-B02-Q09 — Role-Based Permissions

**Interview Question:** How would you design permissions for performance information?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Performance data was sensitive and different personas required different levels of visibility.

**Task:** I needed to protect confidentiality while enabling legitimate performance workflows.

**Action:** I mapped personas, data sensitivity, ownership and business actions, then designed least-privilege access and tested representative scenarios.

**Result:** Users received appropriate access without unnecessary exposure of performance information.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Role-based permissions should distinguish employee, manager, HR, executive and administrative access according to approved governance.

**SME Probe:** How would you test a performance-security model?

### HR-APH3-B02-Q10 — Workflow and Routing

**Interview Question:** What should you consider when designing performance-form workflows?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Performance forms were frequently delayed because routing rules did not reflect the actual organization.

**Task:** I needed to make workflow predictable and resilient.

**Action:** I mapped participants, manager relationships, exceptions, delegation, transfers and organizational changes before configuring routing.

**Result:** Forms moved through the cycle more reliably.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance workflows should reflect approved review stages and account for organizational hierarchy and exceptions.

**SME Probe:** What happens to an in-flight performance form when the employee changes manager?

### HR-APH3-B02-Q11 — Goal-to-Form Integration

**Interview Question:** Why is integration between goals and performance forms important?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Employees had to re-enter goal information during performance reviews.

**Task:** I needed to reduce duplication and improve data continuity.

**Action:** I designed the performance process so approved goals could flow into the appropriate review context, with clear ownership and lifecycle rules.

**Result:** Administrative effort reduced and the review was grounded in previously established objectives.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal information can be surfaced within Performance Management forms according to the configured performance architecture.

**SME Probe:** What governance is needed when goals change after the performance form is launched?

### HR-APH3-B02-Q17 — Configuration vs Customization

**Interview Question:** When would you configure standard Performance & Goals capabilities versus build an extension?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client requested custom behavior because its existing appraisal process was highly specialized.

**Task:** I needed to satisfy the requirement without creating unnecessary technical debt.

**Action:** I first assessed standard configuration, process redesign and acceptable extensions. I used customization only where there was a defensible business requirement and lifecycle case.

**Result:** The solution remained maintainable and aligned with the target architecture.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Standard goal, form, rating, workflow and calibration capabilities should be preferred when they meet the business need.

**SME Probe:** What evidence would justify an extension?

### HR-APH3-B02-Q19 — Product Architecture Trade-Off

**Interview Question:** A client wants every performance requirement configured exactly as its legacy system worked. How would you respond?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Stakeholders equated successful implementation with reproducing every legacy behavior.

**Task:** I needed to prevent legacy design from becoming unnecessary cloud complexity.

**Action:** I classified requirements into business outcomes, policy constraints, user needs and legacy habits, then mapped each to standard capability, process change or justified extension.

**Result:** The target design focused on business value rather than historical system behavior.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would use standard SuccessFactors Performance & Goals capabilities wherever they can satisfy the intended outcome.

**SME Probe:** What is the strongest reason not to replicate legacy functionality?

**Situation:** A client had a configured system but fragmented processes, poor adoption and limited insight.
