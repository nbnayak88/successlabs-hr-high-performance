### HR-APH3-B05-Q01 — Discovering the Real Performance Requirement

**Interview Question:** How would you identify the real business requirement behind a request to “implement Performance Management”?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client asked for a new performance solution because its existing annual appraisal process was unpopular.

**Task:** I needed to understand the underlying business problem rather than treating the product request as the requirement.

**Action:** I interviewed HR, managers and employees, mapped the current process, identified pain points and separated policy, process, people, data and technology issues.

**Result:** We discovered that the primary problems were weak goal quality, infrequent conversations and inconsistent manager practices rather than simply a missing system.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I used Performance & Goals capabilities only after defining the target performance process and its required outcomes.

**SME Probe:** What questions would you ask before discussing configuration?

### HR-APH3-B05-Q02 — Business Outcome

**Interview Question:** How would you convert a vague requirement such as “improve performance management” into a measurable requirement?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Leadership wanted “better performance management” but had no measurable definition.

**Task:** I needed to establish outcome-oriented requirements.

**Action:** I translated the request into measurable outcomes such as improved goal quality, timely check-ins, stronger evidence, greater rating consistency and better employee experience.

**Result:** The transformation had clear success criteria instead of a generic technology objective.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** SuccessFactors capabilities were mapped to measurable process outcomes rather than feature adoption alone.

**SME Probe:** Which KPI would you prioritize for the first release?

### HR-APH3-B05-Q03 — Stakeholder Requirement Conflict

**Interview Question:** What would you do when HR wants a simple process but business leaders request extensive performance detail?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** HR wanted a lightweight employee experience while leaders requested many fields, ratings and approval steps.

**Task:** I needed to reconcile the competing requirements.

**Action:** I classified requirements as mandatory, valuable, optional and unnecessary, then assessed each against business value, employee effort, governance and architecture.

**Result:** We agreed on a simpler core process with only high-value information.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** The Performance form was designed around essential goals, competencies and evidence rather than every requested field.

**SME Probe:** How do you distinguish a genuine requirement from a stakeholder preference?

### HR-APH3-B05-Q04 — Goal Requirement

**Interview Question:** How would you gather requirements for enterprise goal management?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Business units used different definitions of a good goal.

**Task:** I needed to establish a common requirement model.

**Action:** I assessed goal categories, measures, alignment, weighting, ownership, timelines, progress tracking and approval expectations.

**Result:** Goal requirements became consistent enough for enterprise design while allowing justified business variation.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal Management requirements were translated into structured goal attributes and alignment capabilities.

**SME Probe:** Which goal requirements belong in policy rather than configuration?

### HR-APH3-B05-Q05 — Continuous Performance Requirement

**Interview Question:** How would you determine whether an organization actually needs continuous performance capabilities?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client requested continuous performance because it was considered a modern feature.

**Task:** I needed to establish whether it solved a real business problem.

**Action:** I examined conversation frequency, feedback practices, manager behavior, goal volatility and the limitations of the existing annual process.

**Result:** The organization adopted continuous performance where frequent conversations added genuine value.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Continuous Performance capabilities were positioned around defined use cases instead of being enabled simply because they were available.

**SME Probe:** What evidence would justify moving from annual to continuous performance?

### HR-APH3-B05-Q06 — Performance Review Requirement

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The client wanted a standardized review process across multiple countries.

**Task:** I needed to understand what the review must accomplish.

**Action:** I captured eligibility, goals, competencies, evidence, self-assessment, manager assessment, ratings, comments, approvals, calibration and closure requirements.

**Result:** The review process had explicit functional and governance requirements.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** These requirements were mapped to appropriate Performance Management form capabilities.

### HR-APH3-B05-Q07 — Performance Evidence Requirement

**Interview Question:** How would you determine what performance evidence should be captured?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers wanted extensive documentation while employees wanted minimal administration.

**Task:** I needed to define evidence requirements that were useful and proportionate.

**Action:** I focused on evidence needed to support goals, competencies, feedback and meaningful review decisions, then removed low-value administrative capture.

**Result:** The process supported stronger decisions without excessive data collection.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Structured feedback and performance forms were used for relevant evidence while informal information remained outside the governed process.

**SME Probe:** What principle should guide evidence collection?

### HR-APH3-B05-Q08 — Rating Requirement

**Interview Question:** How would you determine the right rating model?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Leaders disagreed between a three-point and five-point rating scale.

**Task:** I needed to identify the business requirement rather than select a scale by preference.

**Action:** I assessed performance philosophy, differentiation needs, manager capability, employee understanding, calibration practices and analytical requirements.

**Result:** The organization selected a rating model aligned to its performance philosophy.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Rating scales were configured only after the business definition and behavioral anchors were approved.

**SME Probe:** Why can adding more rating levels reduce consistency?

### HR-APH3-B05-Q11 — Global and Local Requirements

**Interview Question:** How would you analyze country-specific performance requirements?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Regional HR teams requested different forms, cycles and rating approaches.

**Task:** I needed to identify which variations were genuinely required.

**Action:** I separated enterprise standards from legal, regulatory, cultural and legitimate business requirements and documented each deviation with rationale.

**Result:** The solution supported controlled localization rather than uncontrolled variation.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Global templates and controlled local variants were designed based on approved requirements.

### HR-APH3-B05-Q12 — Requirement Prioritization

**Interview Question:** How would you prioritize a large backlog of performance requirements?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Stakeholders submitted hundreds of requests for the new performance solution.

**Task:** I needed to create a manageable delivery scope.

**Action:** I prioritized requirements by business value, employee impact, compliance, strategic alignment, dependency, complexity and risk.

**Result:** The first release focused on capabilities that materially improved performance outcomes.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Core goal, performance-review and continuous-performance capabilities were prioritized before lower-value enhancements.

**SME Probe:** How would you handle a high-value requirement with a major dependency?

### HR-APH3-B05-Q14 — Data and Reporting Requirements

**Interview Question:** How would you gather requirements for performance reporting?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Executives requested dashboards without defining the decisions they needed to make.

**Task:** I needed to prevent dashboard-driven requirements.

**Action:** I started with business decisions, defined KPIs and dimensions, identified source data and agreed calculation definitions before designing reports.

**Result:** Reporting requirements became decision-oriented and traceable.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance reporting requirements were linked to governed definitions for goals, ratings, completion and cycle metrics.

### HR-APH3-B05-Q15 — Process Exception Requirements

**Interview Question:** How would you capture exception requirements for employees who change managers during a performance cycle?

**Action:** I documented scenarios for manager changes, transfers, leave, new hires, terminations and organizational moves, including eligibility and ownership rules.

**Result:** Exception handling became part of the designed process rather than an operational workaround.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance workflows and employee context were designed to account for lifecycle changes originating from Employee Central.

### HR-APH3-B05-Q17 — Requirements Traceability

**Interview Question:** How would you maintain traceability from business requirements to configuration and testing?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Previous projects had requirements, configuration and test cases stored separately.

**Task:** I needed to establish end-to-end traceability.

**Action:** I created a requirement ID structure connecting business outcome, process requirement, solution capability, configuration, integration, test case and acceptance evidence.

**Result:** Stakeholders could trace each important requirement through delivery.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Each major Performance & Goals requirement was linked to the relevant goal plan, form, workflow, permission, report or integration and corresponding test.

**SME Probe:** What should happen when a requirement cannot be traced to a delivered capability?
