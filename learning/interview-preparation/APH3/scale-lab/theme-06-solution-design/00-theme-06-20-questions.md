### HR-APH3-B06-Q01 — Designing the Target Performance Solution

**Interview Question:** How would you design a target Performance & Goals solution for a global organization?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A global organization had fragmented appraisal processes and inconsistent employee experiences.

**Task:** I needed to design a target solution that connected strategy, goals, conversations, reviews and business outcomes.

**Action:** I designed the journey as Strategy → Expectations → Goals → Check-ins → Feedback → Evidence → Review → Calibration → Development Follow-up, then mapped business, process, data, application, integration, security and experience requirements.

**Result:** The organization received a coherent target-state design rather than a collection of product features.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal Management, Continuous Performance, Performance Management and calibration capabilities were positioned within the broader HR architecture.

**SME Probe:** What should be decided before selecting specific SuccessFactors configuration?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q02 — Process-to-Solution Alignment

**Interview Question:** How would you ensure the solution reflects the approved performance process?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A previous implementation had configured forms before the business process was finalized.

**Task:** I needed to prevent technology from dictating process.

**Action:** I mapped each process stage to required capabilities, actors, information, decisions and controls before mapping to product functionality.

**Result:** The solution design remained traceable to business outcomes.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Process stages were mapped to goal plans, performance forms, continuous-performance capabilities and calibration where appropriate.

**SME Probe:** What is the risk of designing from product screens backward?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q03 — Goal Architecture

**Interview Question:** How would you design an enterprise goal architecture?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Business units created goals using inconsistent structures and terminology.

**Task:** I needed to create a scalable goal model.

**Action:** I defined enterprise goal categories, alignment relationships, measures, ownership, weighting, timelines, progress states and governance while minimizing unnecessary complexity.

**Result:** Goals became comparable and easier to manage across the enterprise.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal Management was designed around standardized goal attributes and controlled alignment/cascading.

**SME Probe:** How much goal standardization is enough?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q04 — Continuous Performance Design

**Interview Question:** How would you design continuous performance without creating excessive administrative work?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Leaders wanted continuous feedback but managers were already overloaded.

**Task:** I needed to design a lightweight experience.

**Action:** I focused continuous interactions on meaningful moments—goal progress, feedback, recognition and course correction—and avoided duplicating formal review activities.

**Result:** Continuous performance became part of work rather than an additional administrative cycle.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Continuous Performance capabilities were designed to complement, not duplicate, formal Performance Management.

**SME Probe:** What should not be captured in a continuous-performance process?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q05 — Performance Form Design

**Interview Question:** What principles would you use when designing a performance form?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A client wanted a very detailed annual form with numerous sections.

**Task:** I needed to balance evidence quality with employee usability.

**Action:** I included only information needed for goals, competencies, evidence, assessment, development conversation and required governance, using clear section ownership and workflow.

**Result:** The form became easier to complete and more useful for the performance conversation.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance Management forms were structured around approved goals, competencies, ratings, comments and relevant workflow.

**SME Probe:** How do you identify fields that should be removed?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q06 — Rating Architecture

**Interview Question:** How would you design a rating architecture for a global performance process?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Different countries used different rating philosophies.

**Task:** I needed to create enterprise consistency without ignoring legitimate local needs.

**Action:** I established common rating definitions, behavioral anchors, governance and effective dates, with controlled local variation where justified.

**Result:** Ratings became more understandable and comparable.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** SuccessFactors rating scales were configured to reflect approved enterprise definitions.

**SME Probe:** Why are rating definitions more important than the number of rating levels?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q07 — Calibration Solution Design

**Interview Question:** How would you design calibration into the performance architecture?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Managers applied ratings inconsistently across teams.

**Task:** I needed to support structured leadership calibration without turning it into an automatic rating algorithm.

**Action:** I defined the population, information available, participant permissions, decision rights, workflow and audit expectations.

**Result:** Calibration became a governed decision process.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Calibration capabilities were positioned after manager assessment and before final communication, consistent with the approved process.

**SME Probe:** What should remain a human decision in calibration?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q08 — Role-Based Experience

**Interview Question:** How would you design different performance experiences for employees, managers and HR?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** All users were presented with the same complex performance information.

**Task:** I needed to improve usability without creating multiple conflicting processes.

**Action:** I mapped role-specific tasks, information and decisions and designed appropriate views, permissions and workflow responsibilities.

**Result:** Each stakeholder saw what was relevant to their role while using the same underlying process.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Role-Based Permissions and role-appropriate workflows supported differentiated experiences.

**SME Probe:** How do you prevent role-specific UX from fragmenting the process?

**Task:** I needed to preserve a common architecture.

### HR-APH3-B06-Q10 — Integration Architecture

**Interview Question:** How would you design Performance & Goals integration with Employee Central?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Performance forms contained outdated employee and manager information.

**Task:** I needed to establish reliable organizational context.

**Action:** I defined Employee Central as the authoritative source for employee and organizational context and designed controlled consumption of required attributes.

**Result:** Performance processes used more reliable employee and organizational information.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Employee Central provides foundational worker and organizational context for Performance & Goals.

**SME Probe:** Why should Performance & Goals not maintain its own employee master?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q11 — Adjacent Talent Integration

**Interview Question:** How would you design the boundary between Performance & Goals and Succession, Learning and Compensation?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Business leaders wanted one HR process to own all talent decisions.

**Task:** I needed to create clear architecture boundaries.

**Action:** I defined APH3 as the owner of performance information and connected it through governed interfaces to AGL4, Learning and ARP5 without transferring process ownership.

**Result:** The architecture enabled integration without creating overlapping responsibilities.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance outcomes can be governed inputs to adjacent processes, while Succession, Learning and Compensation retain their own decision domains.

**SME Probe:** Why is data sharing not the same as process ownership?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q12 — Security-by-Design

**Interview Question:** How would you design security into Performance & Goals from the beginning?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization treated security as a configuration task near go-live.

**Task:** I needed to make confidentiality part of the solution architecture.

**Action:** I classified performance information, mapped roles and access needs, designed permissions and visibility rules, and included audit and privacy requirements.

**Result:** Security became an architectural property rather than a late-stage control.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Role-Based Permissions and form-level visibility controls were aligned to approved access requirements.

**SME Probe:** What performance information should receive the strongest access restrictions?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q14 — Exception Architecture

**Action:** I modeled new hires, transfers, manager changes, leave and termination scenarios and defined eligibility, ownership, routing and closure behavior.

**Result:** Exceptions became designed process paths instead of manual intervention.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Employee Central lifecycle information was used to support appropriate performance-process handling.

### HR-APH3-B06-Q15 — Analytics Architecture

**Interview Question:** How would you design performance analytics without encouraging unhealthy rating comparisons?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Executives wanted a dashboard ranking every manager by average rating.

**Task:** I needed to provide insight without creating misleading or harmful measures.

**Action:** I assessed business purpose, data quality, population comparability, statistical context and behavioral consequences before defining KPIs.

**Result:** Analytics focused on meaningful performance patterns rather than simplistic ranking.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Performance reporting was designed around governed metrics such as goal progress, completion, rating distributions and cycle effectiveness.

**SME Probe:** What makes a performance KPI potentially dangerous?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q16 — Employee Experience Architecture

**Interview Question:** How would you design a performance experience employees actually want to use?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Employees considered the existing annual review process bureaucratic.

**Task:** I needed to reduce friction while preserving performance rigor.

**Action:** I simplified navigation, reduced unnecessary fields, surfaced relevant goals, enabled timely feedback and aligned tasks to natural moments in the employee journey.

**Result:** The performance experience became more usable and conversation-oriented.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Goal Management and Continuous Performance capabilities were organized around employee and manager tasks rather than system-centric navigation.

**SME Probe:** What is the difference between usability and a genuinely better performance experience?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q17 — Standard vs Custom

**Interview Question:** How would you decide whether a performance requirement should use standard SuccessFactors capability or customization?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A business unit requested extensive customization because its legacy system behaved differently.

**Task:** I needed to protect maintainability while meeting legitimate business needs.

**Action:** I evaluated the business outcome, standard capability, process simplification opportunity, integration impact, upgrade risk and total cost before considering extensions.

**Result:** Most requirements were addressed through standard capabilities and process redesign.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Standard Goal Management, Performance Management and Continuous Performance capabilities were preferred before custom solutions.

**SME Probe:** When is customization genuinely justified?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q18 — Performance Architecture Roadmap

**Interview Question:** How would you design a phased Performance & Goals implementation?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** The organization wanted a complete transformation but had limited change capacity.

**Task:** I needed to sequence capabilities without creating architectural rework.

**Action:** I prioritized foundational employee data, goals and core performance processes first, followed by continuous performance, calibration, analytics and advanced capabilities based on readiness.

**Result:** The organization could deliver value incrementally while preserving the target architecture.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** A phased roadmap can establish Goal Management and Performance Management foundations before expanding into continuous performance and advanced analytics.

**SME Probe:** What should never be deferred from the architectural foundation?

### HR-APH3-B06-Q19 — Future AI Readiness

**Interview Question:** How would you design Performance & Goals so it can support future AI capabilities?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Leadership wanted AI-assisted performance insights but foundational data was inconsistent.

**Task:** I needed to avoid introducing AI before the information architecture was ready.

**Action:** I prioritized clean goals, structured evidence, governed ratings, clear data ownership, privacy controls and explainable analytics before considering AI use cases.

**Result:** The organization created a stronger foundation for responsible AI adoption.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** Business AI capabilities should consume governed performance information with appropriate human oversight, permissions and privacy controls.

**SME Probe:** Why is data quality a prerequisite for responsible performance AI?

**Situation:** Executives needed a concise view of a complex transformation.
