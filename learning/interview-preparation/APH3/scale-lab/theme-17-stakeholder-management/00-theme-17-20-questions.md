### HR-APH3-B17-Q01 — Conflicting Stakeholder Priorities
### Interview Question
How would you manage HR, business leaders and managers who have conflicting priorities for Performance & Goals?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** HR wanted strong governance, business leaders wanted faster completion, and managers wanted a simpler review process.

**Task:** I needed to align the stakeholders around a workable target state.

**Action:** I separated requirements from preferences, mapped each request to employee, manager and business outcomes, identified non-negotiable controls, and facilitated a decision using agreed criteria.

**Result:** The team reached a common design direction without allowing the loudest stakeholder to determine the solution.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would prioritize Performance & Goals requirements across goal setting, continuous feedback, reviews and calibration using business value, employee experience, control and feasibility.

**SME Probe:** What would you do if the senior-most stakeholder rejects the agreed prioritization?

### HR-APH3-B17-Q02 — Executive Sponsor Alignment
### Interview Question
How would you keep an executive sponsor aligned during a Performance & Goals transformation?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** An executive sponsor wanted rapid visible progress while the project team was still resolving design dependencies.

**Task:** I needed to maintain confidence without overstating readiness.

**Action:** I translated technical progress into business outcomes, maintained a concise decision log, highlighted risks early and presented clear choices rather than technical detail.

**Result:** The sponsor remained informed and could make timely decisions without micromanaging solution design.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would report progress using metrics such as review-cycle readiness, adoption, manager experience, goal quality and critical risks.

**SME Probe:** How would you communicate a serious design issue to an executive without creating unnecessary alarm?

### HR-APH3-B17-Q03 — HR and Business Disagreement
### Interview Question
What would you do if HR and business leadership disagreed about the performance-management model?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** HR wanted a standardized enterprise process while a business unit wanted a highly customized performance process.

**Task:** I needed to determine where standardization was valuable and where differentiation was justified.

**Action:** I compared the two models against strategic objectives, regulatory needs, employee experience, reporting, maintainability and implementation complexity.

**Result:** The stakeholders agreed on a common core with controlled exceptions instead of competing designs.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would use a common Performance & Goals architecture and permit justified variations only where business or regulatory requirements require them.

**SME Probe:** What evidence would justify a deviation from the global template?

**Situation:** Managers perceived the new process as additional administrative work.

### HR-APH3-B17-Q08 — Requirement Challenge
### Interview Question
How would you challenge a stakeholder requirement without damaging the relationship?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** A stakeholder requested a complex customization based on a historical process.

**Task:** I needed to determine whether the requirement was genuinely necessary.

**Action:** I asked what business outcome the requirement supported, quantified its value and compared standard, simplified and customized options.

**Result:** The stakeholder accepted a simpler solution because the conversation focused on outcome rather than rejecting the request.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would challenge unnecessary Performance & Goals complexity by tracing each requirement to a business or employee outcome.

**SME Probe:** What language do you use when saying “no” to a stakeholder?

### HR-APH3-B17-Q09 — Steering Committee Escalation
### Interview Question
How would you handle an unresolved Performance & Goals design issue at steering committee level?

### STAR Answer
**Situation:** Two senior stakeholder groups could not agree on a critical review-process decision.

**Task:** I needed to enable an executive decision rather than simply escalate the disagreement.

**Action:** I summarized the decision, options, business impacts, risks, dependencies and recommendation, then identified the specific decision required from the steering committee.

**Result:** The committee could decide quickly because the issue was framed as a business decision.

**SAP SuccessFactors Performance & Goals Example:** I would escalate major choices involving review design, calibration, governance or enterprise standardization with clear impact analysis.

**SME Probe:** What should never be escalated to steering committee?

**Situation:** Stakeholders were receiving too much information but still missing important decisions.
